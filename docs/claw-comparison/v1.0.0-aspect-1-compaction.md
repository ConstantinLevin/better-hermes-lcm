**Nine improvements are worth porting.** The strongest are incomplete-response detection, atomic publication, and correct frontier queries. Claw’s compression targets and depth policy are not suitable replacements for B’s weighted tuning.

This is a read-only audit of the supplied code and relevant tests, supplemented by isolated, in-memory probes. No files were modified; full test suites and live summarizer benchmarks were not run. Below, **A** means lossless-claw and **B** means betterlcm. Effort ratings are S—localized changes, M—a module plus integration hooks, L—storage/lifecycle work.

1. **Detect provider-truncated summaries before accepting them.**

   **Claw:** Recognizes completion signals including `length`, `max_tokens`, and `incomplete`, including nested response envelopes. A nonempty but incomplete first response triggers a retry with increased completion headroom. Its test explicitly covers `choices[0].finish_reason = "length"`. [A src/summarize.ts:786](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/summarize.ts:786), [A src/summarize.ts:1044](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/summarize.ts:1044), [A src/summarize.ts:1829](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/summarize.ts:1829), [A test/summarize.test.ts:850](lossless-claw v1.0.0 test/summarize.test.ts:850)

   **B:** Reads `response.choices[0].message.content`, sanitizes reasoning, and returns the text without checking the completion’s finish reason. Escalation accepts it if it is shorter than `source_tokens`. An isolated probe of that response reader returned partial text carrying `finish_reason="length"` as an ordinary summary. [B escalation.py:231](escalation.py:231), [B escalation.py:582](escalation.py:582)

   **Why it matters:** Removing deterministic L3 truncation does not prevent truncation at the provider’s output limit. The lost ending may contain the remaining topics or the expansion index. Raw provenance survives, but the visible index can conceal what lies underneath.

   **Port:** Add a fork `summary_response.py` helper, called from `_call_llm_for_summary`, that returns text plus completion status. Retry incomplete output within the existing spend/deadline budget; reject persistently incomplete results through `SummaryUnavailableError`. Keep B’s no-truncation failure path—Claw’s retry mechanism is useful, but its eventual fallback is not. Test truncated prose, truncated expansion lists, successful recovery, repeated truncation, and cancellation in `tests/fork/test_summary_response.py`.

   **Effort/value: S / very high.** Applies at every window without changing successful default compaction.

2. **Commit the node, metadata, and frontier advance as one publication transaction.**

   **Claw:** Its leaf pass inserts the summary, links source messages, and replaces the active context range inside one transaction. Condensation similarly publishes the parent and its edges together with the frontier replacement. [A src/compaction.ts:2324](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:2324), [A src/compaction.ts:2450](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:2450)

   **B:** Leaf publication calls `add_node`, writes `node_meta`, invokes follow-up hooks, and then advances the raw frontier. `add_node` commits immediately; `node_meta.write` commits separately; lifecycle state uses another SQLite connection. Condensed nodes also commit before their sidecar metadata. [B compaction.py:1019](compaction.py:1019), [B dag.py:250](dag.py:250), [B node_meta.py:84](node_meta.py:84), [B lifecycle_state.py:68](lifecycle_state.py:68), [B engine.py:5856](engine.py:5856)

   **Why it matters:** A crash between commits can expose an L2 summary without its L2 marker, or leave a committed leaf with an unadvanced raw checkpoint. B’s provenance IDs are already embedded in the node, so this is **not** a claim that its source edges disappear; the defect is inconsistent publication and possible repeat coverage. Assembly omits the level tag when metadata is absent. [B engine.py:6320](engine.py:6320)

   **Port:** Introduce `compaction_publish.py`. Use one connection and transaction for node insertion, sidecar insertion, and conditional lifecycle advancement. Add transaction-aware internal helpers to `dag.py`, `node_meta.py`, and `lifecycle_state.py`; update in-memory cursors only after commit. Keep slow summarization outside the transaction. Route both publication hooks through it.

   Test failures after every write, concurrent readers, rollback, and restart recovery in `tests/fork/test_compaction_publish.py`; extend the existing metadata round-trip tests. [B tests/fork/test_node_meta.py:54](tests/fork/test_node_meta.py:54)

   **Effort/value: M / very high.** A correctness improvement at both anchors; successful DAG structure stays unchanged.

3. **Prepare durable hidden summaries before pressure arrives, then validate and publish them.**

   **Claw:** Below-threshold leaf pressure can schedule preparation with `publishPolicy: "prepare-only"`. Foreground publication uses `publish-ready-only`, which cannot claim work or call the summarizer. Before publishing, Claw checks source fingerprints, range continuity, and child validity under its publication lock. Publication promotes the required ancestors and replaces the frontier transactionally.    **B:** Its lookahead starts inside `compress()`, stores results in process-local futures, and publishes them sequentially during that same call. The compaction lock is per-engine and process-local. B already has an appropriate background-publication design document, but its contract tests explicitly remain `xfail` because implementation has not landed.    **Why it matters:** Parallelism shortens a foreground pause; hidden preparation can remove much of that pause. Durable results also survive restart or a failed sibling.    **Port:** Implement B’s existing design through fork `pending_compaction.py` and `pending_compaction_store.py`, reusing `leaf_pipeline.py` and finding 2’s publisher. Prepare only stable chunks selected by B’s own algorithm. Fingerprint source IDs/content, session generation, and relevant policy; revalidate before promotion. Start with leaves, keeping B’s condensation decisions in the foreground.

   Preserve the 256k default by giving speculative preparation a zero low-anchor budget; resolve any new preparation budgets through `window_scaling.py`. Add tests under `tests/fork/` for restart reuse, tail growth, changed sources/configuration, superseded workers, failed siblings, and invisibility to assembly/search/expansion. Claw already tests stale claims and changed canonical children. [A test/pending-summary-store.test.ts:237](lossless-claw v1.0.0 test/pending-summary-store.test.ts:237), [A test/pending-summary-coordinator.test.ts:2061](lossless-claw v1.0.0 test/pending-summary-coordinator.test.ts:2061)

   **Effort/value: L / high, especially at 1M.** This is the largest useful architectural port.

4. **Enforce the caller’s wait budget independently of provider timeout semantics.**

   **Claw:** Wraps each provider call in an explicit timer that rejects the waiting promise when the configured duration expires. That is a local elapsed-time bound on waiting for that call. [A src/summarize.ts:262](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/summarize.ts:262), [A src/summarize.ts:1615](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/summarize.ts:1615)

   **B:** `LeafLookahead.take()` calls `future.result()` without a timeout. The serial non-sweep fallback passes no deadline into the leaf summarizer. Even where a deadline is supplied, the resulting timeout value is reused for each fallback route and again for L2. [B leaf_pipeline.py:154](leaf_pipeline.py:154), [B compaction.py:935](compaction.py:935), [B engine.py:1711](engine.py:1711), [B escalation.py:343](escalation.py:343), [B escalation.py:588](escalation.py:588)

   This is not merely hypothetical provider behavior: the installed Hermes auxiliary client documents streaming timeout as an **idle** timeout, with a separate ceiling of `max(600, 4 × timeout)`. Hermes agent/auxiliary_client.py:6124 (Hermes Agent host internals; path elided)

   **Why it matters:** B’s 200-second leaf-loop budget at 1M is checked between operations; it does not itself bound an outstanding future. Long waits delay publication and keep the compaction lock occupied. [B window_scaling.py:69](window_scaling.py:69), [B compaction.py:706](compaction.py:706), [B host_cooldown.py:137](host_cooldown.py:137)

   **Port:** Add a fork deadline helper; pass one monotonic deadline through serial leaves, lookahead, route fallbacks, L2, and condensation. Use bounded future waits, recomputing remaining time before each attempt. Freeze worker inputs so an abandoned worker cannot read a later session’s mutable state; reject late results before publication. Map expiration into B’s existing partial-progress/cooldown path.

   Test a future that never resolves, slow streaming, multiple fallback routes, partial publication, and a late successful result. Extend `test_parallel_leaf_sweep.py` and `test_weighted_chunking.py`.

   **Effort/value: M / high.** Port the local watchdog pattern; Claw’s timer does not cancel the underlying request or prove a whole fallback chain meets one deadline.

5. **Allow condensation when there is no eligible leaf pass.**

   **Claw:** Its full sweep reaches the condensation phase after finding no raw chunk; that phase is not conditional on creating a leaf. The pending planner can likewise build parents entirely from existing canonical summaries. [A src/compaction.ts:1150](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:1150), [A src/compaction.ts:1218](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:1218), [A src/pending-summary-coordinator.ts:454](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/pending-summary-coordinator.ts:454)

   **B:** Preflight eligibility is primarily a raw-backlog check; deferred maintenance also requires raw backlog. More decisively, `_compress_impl` returns from its “no leaf compacted” branch before either condensation path. Directly calling `_maybe_condense()` works, but normal `compress()` does not reach it in this situation. [B compaction.py:295](compaction.py:295), [B engine.py:2159](engine.py:2159), [B compaction.py:1087](compaction.py:1087), [B compaction.py:1162](compaction.py:1162)

   **Why it matters:** Pressure can reside in summaries after an interrupted sweep or a switch to a smaller window. Waiting for another sufficiently large raw chunk leaves compaction unable to address that pressure. An isolated Claw planner probe successfully planned parents over existing summaries alone.

   **Port:** Add a fork `compaction_pressure.py` helper distinguishing eligible raw work from eligible summary work. Hook it into `should_compress_preflight` and the no-leaf return branch. Under actual pressure, permit bounded condensation-only recovery followed by assembly. Keep B’s weighted summary budget and age ordering; do not introduce routine below-threshold condensation.

   Extend `tests/fork/test_condensation.py` through the **public `compress()` path**, covering summaries plus only a protected tail, window reduction, an exhausted previous leaf budget, no eligible parent group, and summarizer failure. Existing fork tests mostly exercise `_maybe_condense()` directly. [B tests/fork/test_condensation.py:63](tests/fork/test_condensation.py:63)

6. **Determine the real frontier before applying any fetch limit.**

   **Claw:** Maintains active context explicitly in `context_items`; compaction selects from that projection. Discovering active depths joins summaries to the projection rather than inferring visibility from a bounded sample of historical nodes. [A src/store/summary-store.ts:845](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/store/summary-store.ts:845), [A src/store/summary-store.ts:857](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/store/summary-store.ts:857), [A src/compaction.ts:1962](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:1962)

   **B:** `_summary_frontier_nodes()` first loads at most 100,000 historical nodes, ordered by depth and creation time, then derives the referenced-child set from that truncated result. Higher-depth parents can therefore be excluded while their already-condensed children are retained. The separate SQL token-total query uses the complete parent set. [B engine.py:5861](engine.py:5861), [B dag.py:463](dag.py:463), [B dag.py:425](dag.py:425)

   **Verified case:** With 100,000 leaves and one parent covering leaves 1–4, B’s selector returned all 100,000 leaves and omitted the parent. The SQL frontier correctly totaled 99,997 one-token nodes.

   **Why it matters:** Condensation can select material that is already covered. The budget loop then subtracts those nodes as though they were removed from the real frontier, producing incorrect progress accounting. This is a historical-corpus limit, not a claim that a 1M prompt necessarily contains 100,000 summaries. [B engine.py:5764](engine.py:5764), [B engine.py:5785](engine.py:5785)

   **Port:** Add a canonical frontier query in `dag.py` using the same complete anti-reference predicate as `get_frontier_token_total`; apply pagination only afterward. Have both condensation selectors consume it through a fork `frontier.py` helper. A full `context_items` migration is unnecessary for this fix.

   Extend `tests/fork/test_hot_paths.py` beyond its current small fixture: exceed 100,000 historical nodes, compare selected IDs with SQL totals, and verify every condensation removes actual frontier nodes. [B tests/fork/test_hot_paths.py:18](tests/fork/test_hot_paths.py:18)

   **Effort/value: S–M / high for unbounded history.** No tuning change is needed.

7. **Bound condensation input by tokens, not only child count.**

   **Claw:** Its classic selector stops adding summaries when the chunk token budget would be exceeded; its pending planner also requires adjacent intervals and checks cumulative input tokens. The classic selector additionally requires enough source tokens to justify condensation. [A src/compaction.ts:1999](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:1999), [A src/compaction.ts:1952](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/compaction.ts:1952), [A src/pending-summary-planner.ts:326](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/pending-summary-planner.ts:326)

   **B:** Both selectors primarily take a fixed number of same-depth nodes. `_condense_summary_nodes` joins their entire summaries and assigns `max(minimum, source_tokens × ratio)` as its output target, without a condensation input cap. The normal target is 40% of input; L1 permits twice that target as completion tokens. [B engine.py:5719](engine.py:5719), [B engine.py:5879](engine.py:5879), [B engine.py:5807](engine.py:5807), [B escalation.py:588](escalation.py:588)

   **Why it matters:** With four children and the nominal 0.4 output ratio, a parent grows to 1.6 times the size of an individual child. Repeated depth increases can therefore create large summarizer requests even though each operation reduces total frontier tokens. B’s fork tests already exercise four 60k-token children as one group, but their mocked summaries do not exercise the corresponding provider input size.    **Port:** Add `condensation_planner.py`, shared by both selectors. Admit children only while serialized input, prompt overhead, and completion reserve fit. Keep B’s oldest-first policy. When a full fan-in will not fit, allow a smaller valid group under pressure; leave sources intact when none fits.

   Put any preferred chunk size in `window_scaling.py`, with a low anchor preserving ordinary upstream groups and a measured 1M anchor. Provider capacity is a separate hard constraint. Do not copy Claw’s fixed 20k ceiling or truncate oversized children. Test real-sized child text, deep DAGs, oversized single children, intermediate windows, and the 256k baseline.

   **Effort/value: M / high for deep DAGs and smaller summarizer models.**

9. **Avoid repeatedly rebuilding and tokenizing the entire selected summary prefix.**

   **Claw:** Resolves each rendered summary’s token cost once, then performs selection using running totals. [A src/assembler.ts:1978](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/assembler.ts:1978), [A src/assembler.ts:1588](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/assembler.ts:1588), [A src/assembler.ts:1626](lossless-claw v1.0.0 src/assembler.ts:1626)

   **B:** With an assembly cap, every candidate executes `join(selected_parts + [part])` and tokenizes that whole growing message. Strings above 32,768 characters bypass its token-count cache. The default assembly cap is disabled, so this is conditional rather than a universal hot-path defect. [B engine.py:6330](engine.py:6330), [B tokens.py:150](tokens.py:150), [B engine.py:6510](engine.py:6510)

   **Verified case:** The actual selection loop, with 100 fitting 8,000-character parts, submitted **40,434,650 characters** to counting calls to produce an **800,693-character** prefix.

   **Why it matters:** Large capped prefixes incur quadratic text-processing volume. Enlarging the LRU does not fix unique growing prefixes that exceed its cacheable-string limit.

   **Port:** Extract the selector into fork `assembly_budget.py`. Use cached rendered-item costs and running accounting, with boundary-aware treatment of B’s joined-message tokenizer and a final exact cap check. Do not simply sum stored `node.token_count`: wrappers, separators, and tokenizer boundaries matter.

   Extend `tests/fork/test_hot_paths.py` with differential selection tests, Unicode and near-budget boundaries, plus a counting-volume regression. Keep uncapped assembly unchanged and require equivalent selection at 256k.

   **Effort/value: M / medium–high when caps are enabled.**
